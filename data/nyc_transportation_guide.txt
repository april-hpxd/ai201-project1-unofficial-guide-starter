NYC Transportation Beyond the Subway: Buses, Bikes, and More
Source: Compiled from r/nyc, r/bikecommuting, MTA rider guides, and student commuter blogs

## NYC Bus System

The NYC bus system is often overlooked in favor of the subway, but it's essential for many trips — especially crosstown travel in Manhattan (the subway runs mostly north-south) and reaching areas poorly served by the subway.

Key bus facts:
- Same fare as subway: $2.90 with OMNY or MetroCard in 2024
- Free transfers between bus and subway within 2 hours
- Select Bus Service (SBS): limited-stop express buses on major corridors (M15 SBS on 1st/2nd Ave, Bx12 SBS on Fordham Rd, etc.); pay at the kiosk before boarding and get a receipt; faster than local bus

Best buses for students to know:
- M15/M15 SBS: runs the entire length of the East Side of Manhattan; essential for students at NYU, Cooper Union, or any East Side school
- M14 SBS: crosstown 14th Street bus; important for East Village to West Village travel
- B63: Park Slope to Bay Ridge Brooklyn; useful for CUNY students in those neighborhoods
- Q train + Queensborough Plaza buses: Queens is primarily served by a mix of subway and bus

Night bus service: all bus routes continue 24/7, though frequency drops significantly (every 20–30 minutes at night vs. every 8–12 minutes during peak). If you're far from a subway at 3am, the bus may be your best option.

## Citi Bike

Citi Bike is NYC's bike share system and one of the best transportation values available to students:
- Monthly membership: $19/month (standard) or approximately $10/month with the NYC Lyft Pink student discount (verify current pricing)
- Rides under 45 minutes (classic bikes) or 30 minutes (electric bikes) are free with membership; time over that charged by the minute
- Over 2,000 stations throughout Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx (expansion ongoing)
- Electric bikes (e-bikes) available in the same dock system; these have a per-minute surcharge with membership but significantly reduce commute time and allow cycling into headwinds or up hills

Citi Bike is most useful for:
- Crosstown trips in Manhattan where the subway is slow
- "Last mile" connections from the subway to your final destination
- Exploring neighborhoods casually
- Exercise integrated into commute (classic bikes only)

Safety: always wear a helmet (legally required for electric bikes; highly recommended for all bikes). NYC has bike lanes throughout much of the city, but many intersections and streets are still challenging for cyclists. The Hudson River Greenway (runs the entire western edge of Manhattan) is the safest cycling route in the city.

## Uber and Lyft

For most students, Uber and Lyft are too expensive for regular use but useful in specific situations:
- Late night when you're too tired or it's too late to feel comfortable on the subway
- When carrying heavy loads (moving furniture, large grocery runs)
- Traveling in groups where the fare split makes it competitive with the subway per person

Typical NYC Uber/Lyft fares: $10–20 for short trips (under 2 miles), $20–40 for medium trips, significantly more in surge pricing. Surge pricing is worst during rain, late-night bar closing times (2am), and major events.

Alternatives: NYC also has dollar vans (informal shared minivans, especially in Brooklyn and Queens neighborhoods), which are technically not fully regulated but are a real part of how people move around in some communities.

## The Staten Island Ferry

The Staten Island Ferry is completely free and operates 24/7. The ferry itself is 25 minutes each way and offers excellent views of the Manhattan skyline and the Statue of Liberty. It's a favorite of students for a free NYC experience and is heavily used by Staten Island residents commuting to Manhattan. From the St. George Terminal on Staten Island, the Staten Island Railway (SIR) connects to points throughout the island, included in the subway fare.

## Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) and Metro-North

If you're commuting from Long Island or Westchester/Connecticut, the commuter rail is essential:
- LIRR: serves Long Island from Penn Station and Atlantic Terminal in Brooklyn; student off-peak fares available; a monthly pass from certain Long Island zones can be $250–400+
- Metro-North: serves Westchester, Rockland, Connecticut; departs from Grand Central Terminal
- NJ Transit: serves New Jersey from Penn Station; useful if you live in Jersey City, Hoboken, Newark, or further NJ suburbs

These are much more expensive than the subway. If you're choosing where to live and cost is a priority, commuting from Long Island via LIRR may not save money over living in a less expensive NYC neighborhood when you factor in the rail pass.

## Walking

In Manhattan especially, walking is often faster than the subway for trips under 20 blocks (roughly 1 mile). Many students underestimate how much walking they'll do in NYC — budget for comfortable, durable shoes as a real necessity. The average NYC resident walks 2–5 miles per day.

A useful rule of thumb: north-south blocks in Manhattan are short (20 per mile); east-west blocks are long (about 4 per mile). So walking 20 blocks north is about 1 mile; walking 4 blocks east is also about 1 mile. When directions say "two avenues over," that's a significant walk.
